Income tax in Nepal can look complicated because your entire salary is not
taxed at one single percentage.

Nepal uses a **progressive income tax system**. As taxable income increases,
different portions of that income fall into different tax bands.

For fiscal year **2083/84 (2026/27)**, Nepal introduced a significantly revised
personal income tax structure for resident individuals.

## Nepal income tax slabs for FY 2083/84

For resident natural persons, the general remuneration tax bands are:

| Annual taxable income | Rate |
| --- | ---: |
| First Rs. 10,00,000 | 1% |
| Next Rs. 5,00,000 | 10% |
| Next Rs. 10,00,000 | 20% |
| Next Rs. 15,00,000 | 27% |
| Above Rs. 40,00,000 | 29% |

The important word here is **progressive**.

If your taxable income reaches a higher slab, the higher rate does **not**
normally apply to all your income. It applies only to the portion inside that
band.

## A simple tax example

Suppose your annual taxable income is:

**Rs. 18,00,000**

Assuming the normal 1% first-band charge applies, the calculation is:

1. First Rs. 10,00,000 at 1% = **Rs. 10,000**
2. Next Rs. 5,00,000 at 10% = **Rs. 50,000**
3. Remaining Rs. 3,00,000 at 20% = **Rs. 60,000**

Total estimated tax:

`Rs. 10,000 + Rs. 50,000 + Rs. 60,000 = Rs. 1,20,000`

Notice that the entire Rs. 18 lakh was **not taxed at 20%**.

Only the portion above Rs. 15 lakh entered the 20% band.

That is the basic idea behind progressive taxation.

## What changed in FY 2083/84?

The FY 2083/84 structure made a major change to personal income tax in Nepal.

Previously, separate basic tax bands existed for individuals assessed as
single and as couples.

For FY 2083/84, the personal income tax slabs for resident natural persons were
combined into a **unified schedule**.

The first band was also increased substantially, while the highest marginal
rate was reduced compared with the previous structure.

<Callout title="Use the correct fiscal year">
Nepal's tax rules can change through the annual Finance Act. A calculator or
article using last year's slabs can produce the wrong result. Always check
which fiscal year a tax estimate is based on.
</Callout>

## What is taxable income?

Your gross salary and your taxable income are not always the same number.

Depending on the rules that apply to you, the calculation may involve items
such as:

- salary and wages
- bonuses
- allowances
- employer-provided benefits
- retirement contributions
- approved deductions
- available rebates or tax credits
- tax already deducted at source

The tax slabs are applied to the **taxable amount after applicable adjustments**,
not simply to whichever number appears as your monthly bank deposit.

## What is the 1% first-band tax?

The first Rs. 10,00,000 falls under a 1% rate in the standard remuneration
schedule for FY 2083/84.

This is associated with Nepal's Social Security Tax framework.

However, the 1% treatment does not apply identically to every taxpayer. For
example, certain taxpayers participating in the contribution-based Social
Security Fund can receive different treatment.

That is one reason a quick slab calculation and your final legal tax liability
may not always be identical.

## Tax slab versus effective tax rate

Your **marginal tax rate** is the rate applied to the highest portion of your
income.

Your **effective tax rate** is:

`Total tax ÷ Total taxable income × 100`

Using the Rs. 18 lakh example:

`Rs. 1,20,000 ÷ Rs. 18,00,000 × 100 ≈ 6.67%`

Even though part of the income falls into the 20% slab, the effective tax rate
is much lower because earlier portions were taxed at lower rates.

This distinction is useful when comparing salary offers or estimating actual
take-home income.

## Monthly salary versus annual tax

Employees are commonly paid monthly, but income tax is based on the relevant
annual taxable income.

A simple estimate may begin with:

`Annual income = Monthly income × 12`

You then account for applicable taxable benefits, deductions and other
adjustments before applying the tax slabs.

Your employer may withhold tax throughout the year rather than waiting until
year-end.

## Why two people with the same salary can pay different tax

Even when two employees receive similar gross salaries, their final tax can
differ.

Possible reasons include:

- retirement contributions
- Social Security Fund participation
- eligible deductions
- insurance-related relief
- applicable rebates
- other taxable income
- benefits and allowances
- tax already withheld

So comparing only gross salary does not always tell you who has the larger
take-home amount.

## Common Nepal tax mistakes

### Applying the highest rate to the entire salary

Progressive tax does not work that way. Each portion of taxable income is
calculated within its applicable slab.

### Using old tax slabs

Nepal's rates can change between fiscal years. Always make sure your
calculation is for **FY 2083/84** if that is the period you are calculating.

### Using gross salary as taxable income automatically

Deductions and adjustments may change the actual taxable amount.

### Ignoring tax already deducted

If tax has already been withheld from salary, that amount matters when
determining what remains payable.

## A sensible way to estimate your Nepal tax

1. Determine your annual income.
2. Identify taxable salary, benefits and other relevant income.
3. Subtract applicable deductions.
4. Determine your taxable income.
5. Apply each FY 2083/84 slab progressively.
6. Apply any relevant credits or special provisions.
7. Compare the result with tax already deducted.

Nepal's income tax system becomes much easier to understand once you stop
thinking of it as one tax percentage applied to your entire salary.

1%, 10%, 20%, 27% and 29% bands.
