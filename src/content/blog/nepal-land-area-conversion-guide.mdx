Land measurement in Nepal can be confusing because the country commonly uses
two traditional systems alongside square feet, square meters, acres and
hectares.

In Kathmandu Valley and many hill areas, land is commonly described in
**Ropani, Aana, Paisa and Daam**. In the Terai, **Bigha, Kattha and Dhur** are
more common.

If you already know the measurement and only need the converted value, use the
[Nepal Land Area Converter](/tools/converters/nepal-land-area-converter).
It accepts both English and Devanagari numerals and shows the same area across
both Nepal customary systems and standard international units.

## Nepal land measurement systems at a glance

### Hill and Kathmandu system

The hill system uses:

- Ropani — **रोपनी**
- Aana — **आना**
- Paisa — **पैसा**
- Daam — **दाम**

The relationships are:

| Unit | Relationship |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 Ropani | 16 Aana |
| 1 Ropani | 64 Paisa |
| 1 Ropani | 256 Daam |
| 1 Aana | 4 Paisa |
| 1 Aana | 16 Daam |
| 1 Paisa | 4 Daam |

The square-foot base relationship used in the calculator is:

**1 Ropani = 5,476 square feet**

That gives:

| Unit | Square feet |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 Ropani / १ रोपनी | 5,476 sq ft |
| 1 Aana / १ आना | 342.25 sq ft |
| 1 Paisa / १ पैसा | 85.5625 sq ft |
| 1 Daam / १ दाम | 21.390625 sq ft |

For compound measurements, use the
[Ropani, Aana, Paisa and Daam converter](/tools/converters/nepal-land-area-converter)
instead of rounding each subdivision separately.

## Terai land measurement system

The Terai system uses:

- Bigha — **बिघा**
- Kattha — **कठ्ठा**
- Dhur — **धुर**

The relationships are:

| Unit | Relationship |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 Bigha | 20 Kattha |
| 1 Kattha | 20 Dhur |
| 1 Bigha | 400 Dhur |

The square-foot base relationship is:

**1 Bigha = 72,900 square feet**

So:

| Unit | Square feet |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 Bigha / १ बिघा | 72,900 sq ft |
| 1 Kattha / १ कठ्ठा | 3,645 sq ft |
| 1 Dhur / १ धुर | 182.25 sq ft |

The [Bigha, Kattha and Dhur converter](/tools/converters/nepal-land-area-converter)
can also show the equivalent Ropani-system measurement, square meters, acres
and hectares.

## How many square feet are in 1 Ropani?

**1 Ropani = 5,476 square feet.**

Using the exact international conversion factor:

`1 sq ft = 0.09290304 m²`

we get:

`5,476 × 0.09290304 = 508.73704704 m²`

So:

**1 Ropani ≈ 508.73704704 square meters.**

For ordinary property comparison you may display fewer decimal places, but a
calculator should avoid rounding the intermediate value before converting to
another unit.

## How many square feet are in 1 Aana?

Because 1 Ropani contains 16 Aana:

`5,476 ÷ 16 = 342.25`

Therefore:

**1 Aana = 342.25 square feet.**

Common Aana values include:

| Aana | Square feet |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 342.25 |
| 2 | 684.50 |
| 4 | 1,369 |
| 8 | 2,738 |
| 12 | 4,107 |
| 16 | 5,476 |

Sixteen Aana becomes one Ropani. For example, **20 Aana** normalizes to
**1 Ropani 4 Aana**.

The [Square Feet to Ropani and Aana converter](/tools/converters/nepal-land-area-converter)
normalizes these values automatically.

## How many square feet are in 1 Paisa and 1 Daam?

One Ropani contains 64 Paisa:

`5,476 ÷ 64 = 85.5625`

So:

**1 Paisa = 85.5625 square feet.**

One Ropani contains 256 Daam:

`5,476 ÷ 256 = 21.390625`

So:

**1 Daam = 21.390625 square feet.**

The subdivision chain is:

`4 Daam = 1 Paisa`

`4 Paisa = 1 Aana`

`16 Aana = 1 Ropani`

## How many square feet are in 1 Bigha?

**1 Bigha = 72,900 square feet.**

It is also equal to:

- 20 Kattha
- 400 Dhur
- approximately 6,772.63 square meters

For a direct comparison with the hill system, use the
[Bigha to Ropani converter](/tools/converters/nepal-land-area-converter).

## How many square feet are in 1 Kattha?

One Bigha contains 20 Kattha:

`72,900 ÷ 20 = 3,645`

Therefore:

**1 Kattha = 3,645 square feet.**

One Kattha contains 20 Dhur.

Common values include:

| Kattha | Square feet |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 3,645 |
| 2 | 7,290 |
| 5 | 18,225 |
| 10 | 36,450 |
| 20 | 72,900 |

## How many square feet are in 1 Dhur?

Because 1 Kattha contains 20 Dhur:

`3,645 ÷ 20 = 182.25`

Therefore:

**1 Dhur = 182.25 square feet.**

Using the square-foot-to-square-meter relationship:

`182.25 × 0.09290304 = 16.93157904`

So:

**1 Dhur ≈ 16.93157904 square meters.**

## Ropani to square feet formula

For a simple whole-Ropani value:

`Square feet = Ropani × 5,476`

For a compound Ropani-Aana-Paisa-Daam measurement:

`Area = (Ropani × 5,476) + (Aana × 342.25) + (Paisa × 85.5625) + (Daam × 21.390625)`

### Example: 2 Ropani 5 Aana 2 Paisa 1 Daam

Calculate each portion:

`2 × 5,476 = 10,952`

`5 × 342.25 = 1,711.25`

`2 × 85.5625 = 171.125`

`1 × 21.390625 = 21.390625`

Total:

**12,855.765625 square feet**

Instead of performing every step manually, enter the compound value in the
[Nepal Ropani calculator](/tools/converters/nepal-land-area-converter).

## Bigha to square feet formula

For Bigha:

`Square feet = Bigha × 72,900`

For a compound Bigha-Kattha-Dhur value:

`Area = (Bigha × 72,900) + (Kattha × 3,645) + (Dhur × 182.25)`

### Example: 1 Bigha 5 Kattha 10 Dhur

`1 × 72,900 = 72,900`

`5 × 3,645 = 18,225`

`10 × 182.25 = 1,822.5`

Total:

**92,947.5 square feet**

The [Bigha, Kattha and Dhur calculator](/tools/converters/nepal-land-area-converter)
can show the normalized customary result and standard-unit equivalents at the
same time.

## Understanding R-A-P-D notation

Land measurements may be written in a compact form such as:

`2-5-2-1`

For the hill system this means:

**2 Ropani – 5 Aana – 2 Paisa – 1 Daam**

The order is always:

`Ropani - Aana - Paisa - Daam`

For example:

`1-4-0-0`

means **1 Ropani 4 Aana**, which is also 20 Aana.

The calculator accepts the component values and returns a normalized
Ropani-Aana-Paisa-Daam result.

## Understanding B-K-D notation

The Terai system uses three components:

`Bigha - Kattha - Dhur`

For example:

`1-5-10`

means **1 Bigha 5 Kattha 10 Dhur**.

Because 20 Dhur becomes one Kattha and 20 Kattha becomes one Bigha, values can
be normalized. For example, **1 Bigha 25 Kattha** becomes **2 Bigha 5 Kattha**.

## Square feet to Ropani

To convert square feet to decimal Ropani:

`Ropani = square feet ÷ 5,476`

For example:

`10,952 ÷ 5,476 = 2`

So:

**10,952 square feet = 2 Ropani.**

For values that do not divide into a whole Ropani, the remaining area can be
expressed as Aana, Paisa and Daam. The
[Square Feet to Ropani converter](/tools/converters/nepal-land-area-converter)
does that normalization for you.

## Square feet to Aana

Because 1 Aana is 342.25 square feet:

`Aana = square feet ÷ 342.25`

For example:

`1,369 ÷ 342.25 = 4`

So:

**1,369 square feet = 4 Aana.**

## Square feet to Kattha and Dhur

For Kattha:

`Kattha = square feet ÷ 3,645`

For Dhur:

`Dhur = square feet ÷ 182.25`

For example:

`7,290 ÷ 3,645 = 2 Kattha`

and:

`1,822.5 ÷ 182.25 = 10 Dhur`

## Ropani to Bigha

Ropani and Bigha belong to different customary systems, so the safest method
is to convert through one precise common area base rather than chaining rounded
values.

Using square feet:

`1 Ropani = 5,476 sq ft`

`1 Bigha = 72,900 sq ft`

Therefore:

`5,476 ÷ 72,900 ≈ 0.0751166 Bigha`

So:

**1 Ropani ≈ 0.0751 Bigha.**

For land records containing Aana, Paisa, Kattha or Dhur as well, convert the
complete compound measurement using the
[Ropani to Bigha calculator](/tools/converters/nepal-land-area-converter).

## Bigha to Ropani

Using the same square-foot bases:

`72,900 ÷ 5,476 ≈ 13.3126`

Therefore:

**1 Bigha ≈ 13.31 Ropani.**

A normalized Ropani-Aana-Paisa-Daam result is often easier to read than a long
decimal Ropani value.

## English and Devanagari numeral support

Nepal property information may appear with Latin or Devanagari numerals.

For example:

`2 Ropani 5 Aana`

and:

`२ रोपनी ५ आना`

represent the same quantities.

Likewise:

`1234.5`

and:

`१२३४.५`

can represent the same number.

Supporting both systems reduces friction when copying values from Nepali
records, notes or property descriptions.

## How to estimate rectangular plot area

For a rectangular plot:

`Area = Length × Width`

A plot measuring **60 ft × 30 ft** has:

`60 × 30 = 1,800 sq ft`

That area can then be converted into Ropani-Aana-Paisa-Daam or
Bigha-Kattha-Dhur.

If you first need to convert dimensions such as meters to feet, use the
[Length Converter](/tools/converters/length-converter).

A length-times-width result is an estimate only when the actual parcel is
irregular. Four boundary lengths by themselves do not always determine the
exact area of an irregular quadrilateral because angles, diagonals or surveyed
coordinates may also matter.

## Common Nepal land conversion mistakes

### Treating Aana as a decimal part of Ropani

Aana is not a base-10 subdivision.

**1 Ropani = 16 Aana**, not 10 Aana.

Therefore, **1 Ropani 8 Aana = 1.5 Ropani** when expressed as decimal Ropani.

### Treating Kattha as a decimal part of Bigha

**1 Bigha = 20 Kattha**, not 10 Kattha.

Therefore, **1 Bigha 10 Kattha = 1.5 Bigha** in decimal Bigha.

### Rounding too early

Keep the full internal value until the final display step. Early rounding can
accumulate when several conversions are chained.

### Mixing the hill and Terai systems

Ropani and Bigha have different subdivision structures. Identify the original
system first, then convert the complete area.

### Confusing length with area

Feet and meters measure length. Square feet and square meters measure area.
Use the [Length Converter](/tools/converters/length-converter) for ordinary
length conversions.
